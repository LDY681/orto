Welcome to the **Open Research Tools for Ophthalmology (ORTO)** research group, dedicated to advancing open science and reproducibility in eye research. Our work focuses on both creating and curating resources. We develop open-source tools for ophthalmic data analysis, actively support their adoption, and facilitate dialogue among researchers, clinicians, industry partners, and other stakeholders. Through this exchange, we aim to promote best practices, align methodologies, and encourage the responsible reuse of data and technologies developed by others in the field.

The name ORTO, the Italian word for a vegetable garden, serves as a guiding metaphor for our approach. We envision open science as a thriving ecosystem in which knowledge, tools, and data are cultivated collectively. Just as a garden flourishes when resources are shared and carefully maintained, scientific progress accelerates when research outputs are openly accessible, continuously refined, and built upon by a diverse community of contributors.

A central component of ORTO is the establishment of a shared repository that includes data analysis software, ophthalmic imaging datasets, and experimental protocols. This resource is designed to support the standardisation of imaging, analysis, and reporting across instruments, institutions, and studies. By doing so, we aim to encourage robust multi-site collaborations and accelerate the discovery of reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis, disease monitoring, and treatment response in ocular conditions.

In addition to tool development and data sharing, we provide curated collections of peer-reviewed, highly influential publications, as well as emerging and niche research topics. These resources are intended to support both newcomers and experienced researchers in navigating and contributing to open science in ophthalmology.

Through tool-building, knowledge sharing, and active community engagement, ORTO seeks to cultivate a sustainable and collaborative research landscape where innovation in eye research can grow openly and collectively.
