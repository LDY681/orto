# OCTAVA – Optical Coherence Tomography Angiography Vascular Analyser
**OCTAVA** is an open-source software designed for the automated extraction and quantitative analysis of OCTA-derived microvascular metrics from en-face images downloaded from the OCTA camera. OCTAVA provides a user-friendly graphical interface that facilitates detailed, non-invasive analysis of retinal, optic nerve head, and skin blood vessels, which is crucial for studying and diagnosing vascular diseases. The software does not provide automated image quality assessment; <span className="text-red-500">users are therefore responsible for evaluating image quality independently.</span>

# How to Use OCTAVA
OCTAVA Graphical User Interface (GUI) is an intuitive interface featuring buttons, dropdown menus, and real-time visualisation panels for seamless user interaction.

The user first selects the image type for analysis: Retina, optic nerve head (ONH), or skin and insert image size The image is then processed with a series of image-processing techniques to generate OCTA microvascular metrics and, where applicable, foveal avascular zone metrics.

First, vessels in the image are segmented using filtering and binarisation techniques. Filtering enhances vessel visibility while reducing noise, improving overall image quality. # OCTAVA OUTPUT
## OCTA-derived Microvascular Metrics
OCTAVA can calculate multiple OCTA-derived metrics, summarised in the table below. The metrics are saved in an Excel file to facilitate easy compilation of data from multiple subjects. When batch processing is selected, multiple input images can be analysed automatically. For each image, a separate output folder is created to store the corresponding results. Additionally, a summary Excel file is generated that consolidates the output data from all processed images.
<span className="text-red-500">For sub-region analysis, if the analysed image is de-centred and the sector is lacking more than 20% of the defined sub-region, the metrics for that sector are marked as NA.</span>

| Metric (abbreviation) | Unit  | Analysed image | Description and importance | Illustration |
| :-------- | :---- | :------ | :------------ | :------ |
Vessel area density (VAD)	| *%*	| Binarised	| Perfused blood vessel area (white pixels) in the binarised OCTA MIP image divided by total image area (all pixels) multiplied by 100. Can be used to demonstrate ischemia. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_1.png" alt="illustration 1" width={200} height={200} />
Vessel length density (VLD) |	*%* |	Skeletonised	| Total vessel length multiplied by the width of one pixel (i.e., total area of skeletonised vessels) divided by the total image area and multiplied by 100. Length measurements are calculated from the skeletonised OCTA MIP image. More sensitive than VAD to perfusion changes at the capillary level.	| <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_2.png" alt="illustration 2" width={200} height={200} />
Mean, median, and distribution of vessel diameter	| $$μm$$	| Binarised |	Mean, median, and distribution of diameter measurements are acquired using a Euclidian distance transform applied to the binarised OCTA MIP. The mean (median) diameter is the mean (median) for a single vessel, including one value for each identified segment. Can inform on dilation, sprouting, or vessel regression.	| <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_3.png" alt="illustration 3" width={200} height={200} />
Total, mean, median, and distribution of vessel length (VL)	| $$μm$$	| Skeletonised	| Total, mean, median, and distribution of the single-vessel centreline lengths including one value for each identified segment measured from the skeletonised image. Network interconnectivity and branching patterns can indicate oxygenation/nutrient delivery dysfunction. 	| <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_4.png" alt="illustration 4" width={200} height={200} />
Branchpoint density (BD)	| $$nodes/mm$$	| Skeletonised	| Number of identified nodes divided by total vessel length. May indicate resilience to occlusion or blockage of blood flow. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_5.png" alt="illustration 5" width={200} height={200} /> 	
Mean (MeanT) and distribution of vessel tortuosity	| *1*	| Skeletonised	| **Arc-over-chord ratio (AOC)**, also called the distance metric, is the simplest and most common method to measure tortuosity.  AOC is defined as the ratio between the true vessel length, measured along the centerline of the skeletonized vessel, and the direct Euclidian distance between the two endpoints. Can inform on pathological microvascular remodelling (vessel excessive bending, twisting or winding) and/or ischemia. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_6.png" alt="illustration 6" width={200} height={200} />	
|	| *1*	| Skeletonised	| **Peak-weighted arc over chord length ratio** tortuosity metric measures how twisty a vessel is by combining how much longer it is than a straight line with the number of bends along its path. | 
|	| *1*	| Skeletonised	| **Sum of angles metric (SOAM),** measures how tortuous a vessel is by adding up all the small turning angles along its path and then dividing by its total length to give a normalized measure of how much it bends. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_7.png" alt="illustration 7" width={200} height={200} />	
Fractal dimension ($D_F$)	| *1*	| Binarised	| Indication of how the network fills space on variable length scales, calculated using the box counting method. FD is commonly reported as $$D_{F} ± D_{Ferror}$$. Altered spatial distribution of the capillary network is an indicator of impairment of oxygenation and nutrient delivery. OCTAVA box-range for image size 1,000 ×1,000 pixels is 4, 8, 12, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_8.png" alt="illustration 8" width={200} height={200} />	
Foveal avascular zone area (FAZA)	| $$mm^2$$	| Binarised	| Total area of black pixels enclosed by the FAZ segmentation contour. Changes in the FAZA can be indicative of macular oedema or ischemia and changes in retinal thickness. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_9.png" alt="illustration 9" width={200} height={200} /> 
FAZ perimeter	| $$mm$$	| Binarised	| Length of the perimeter of the FAZ segmentation contour. It provides an additional metric about the change in FAZA. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_10.png" alt="illustration 10" width={200} height={200} />
FAZ axis ratio	| *1*	| Binarised	| Ratio between the major and minor axis of the ellipsoidal best fit to FAZ outline shape. Elongation of the FAZA can be indicative of changes in retinal thickness. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_11.png" alt="illustration 11" width={200} height={200} />
FAZ circularity	| *1*	| Binarised	| FAZ area multiplied by 4π divided by FAZ perimeter squared. Indicates elongation of the FAZ, which can be indicative of changes in retinal thickness.	| 
FAZ acircularity index	| *1*	| Binarised	| Ratio between the measured perimeter and the perimeter of a circular area of the same size. Indication of elongation of the FAZ. | <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_12.png" alt="illustration 12" width={200} height={200} />
Foveal avascular density 300 (FD-300)	| *%*	| Binarised	| Percentage of the area occupied by vessels (white pixels) divided by the area in a 300-μm width rim surrounding the FAZ. This rim width value was originally chosen based on the relationship between FAZ and ganglion cell complex thickness in normal eyes to better distinguish between normal variations in FAZ and those due to pathology.	| <ThumbNail src="/static/images/octava/illustration_13.png" alt="illustration 13" width={200} height={200} />
